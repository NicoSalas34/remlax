\section{Validation}\label{sec:validation}

Validation is organised around what a failure would have meant, not around
test counts. Four levels are used. The first two compare the solver with
itself; the third with a computation beholden to neither engine; the fourth
with four programs written by other people. The table of every check, with
the printed values and the git revision tested, is regenerated from the logs
by two scripts and published as \emph{docs/validation.md}; the run reported
here is that of 2026-09-28 on revision \texttt{f576638} plus that day's
working tree: 399 checks, 387 passed, the 12 failures being three models
discussed below.

\subsection{Internal agreement}

\emph{tests/R/test\_remlax\_tmb\_parity.R} checks that the two engines read
$\theta$ identically: that $\Sigma(\theta)$ agrees for \code{iid},
\code{diag} and \code{us}; that the closed-form \code{ar1} and \code{ar1ar1}
precisions are exact inverses of the correlation matrices the dense engine
builds, verified by forming $K^{-1}K$ rather than by inspecting a sparsity
pattern; and that the analytic log-determinants agree with numerical ones.
Thirty checks, largest discrepancy $4.4 \times 10^{-16}$. The dense engine
itself is checked against closed forms in \emph{tests/python}: parameter
counts and positive semi-definiteness of every $\Sigma$ structure, the
automatic gradient against central finite differences ($2 \times 10^{-8}$
relative), the assembled $V$ against a direct Kronecker computation
($5 \times 10^{-15}$), the balanced one-factor REML against its analytic
solution ($4 \times 10^{-9}$), and 169 unit tests, one per feature, each
against an independent numpy reference.

\subsection{Cross-engine agreement, in both directions}

\emph{tests/R/test\_remlax\_tmb\_vs\_dense.R} evaluates each engine at the
other's optimum. Both directions are needed: an engine could have a correct
surface along the other's path and be wrong elsewhere. Sixteen checks over
four designs agree to $2.3 \times 10^{-13}$ on cross-evaluation and
$10^{-4}$ on the attained optima.

\subsection{An arbiter beholden to neither engine}

Two engines that agree prove less than one engine checked against an
independent computation. \emph{tests/R/test\_remlax\_tmb\_prec.R} carries a
REML likelihood written out in dense linear algebra, and it settled three
disagreements during development that engine-to-engine comparison could not.
The defect it caught in the precision path is the class of defect an
estimate-based test cannot see: a missing $\log|K|$, constant in $\theta$, left
the gradient and the estimates untouched and made every likelihood ratio and
information criterion wrong.

The same lesson returned on 2026-09-28 from the other side. Comparing
\code{exp} with \pkg{nlme}'s \code{corExp} showed that every metric
structure written as $\rho^{d}$ started the optimiser at $\rho = 0$ exactly,
a point where the gradient is zero by construction, so that L-BFGS-B declared
convergence without moving; the earlier check of \code{exp} against
\code{own} had compared two fits by the same engine from the same start. The
start was moved to $\rho = 0.5$ at the median spacing of the coordinates, and
the comparison with \pkg{nlme} now agrees to $10^{-9}$ on $-2\ell$.

\subsection{Agreement with established software}

Against \pkg{asreml} 4.2 \citep{asreml}, \pkg{lme4} 2.0 \citep{lme4},
\pkg{sommer} 4.4 \citep{sommer} and \pkg{nlme} 3.1 \citep{nlme}, in a
container where all are installed, each comparison is the same model on the
same simulated data. Agreement is required first on the restricted
log-likelihood at the optimum, in each program's own constant: $10^{-6}$
absolute against \pkg{lme4} and \pkg{nlme}, which carry the full REML
constant; $10^{-5}$ against \pkg{asreml}, which omits $(n-p)\log(2\pi)/2$ and
stops on its own criterion; differences between nested models only against
\pkg{sommer}, whose reported value carries a data-dependent constant. Then on
variance components ($10^{-4}$ relative, $10^{-3}$ for \pkg{asreml}), fixed
effects and their standard errors ($10^{-6}$ and $10^{-4}$), BLUPs
($10^{-4}$) and prediction error variances against the mixed-model-equations
formula written out by hand ($1.6 \times 10^{-14}$; \pkg{lme4}'s
\code{condVar} follows a different convention, without the uncertainty on
$\beta$, and is checked against its own formula).

The models covered are: one random factor on three layouts, two and three
crossed factors on balanced and unbalanced layouts, nesting, correlated and
independent random slopes, groups of size one to twelve (\pkg{lme4}, 58
checks); a genomic relationship matrix on one term with BLUPs and prediction
error variances, three traits with unstructured and diagonal covariances and
the likelihood ratio between them, additive plus dominance matrices on the
same factor, a multi-environment design (\pkg{sommer}, 22 checks);
\code{corAR1}, \code{corCAR1}, AR2, ARMA(1,1), compound symmetry,
exponential, Gaussian, linear and spherical spatial correlation, an
unstructured covariance written as \code{corSymm} with \code{varIdent}, and a
random intercept with an AR1 residual (\pkg{nlme}, 61 checks, largest
$-2\ell$ gap $9 \times 10^{-9}$); AR1, separable AR1$\times$AR1, two-dimensional
splines, a covariance shared between two terms, heritability with its
delta-method standard error, Wald tests, the four saturated
parameterisations, the linear-variance and anisotropic Mat\'ern kernels,
user-written kernels, sectioned residuals, prediction with standard errors,
and Kenward--Roger denominator degrees of freedom against \pkg{pbkrtest}
(\pkg{asreml}, 65 checks, largest log-likelihood gap $2.9 \times 10^{-8}$).
A further suite adds ar2, ar3, ma1, ma2, arma, sar, uniform, banded and
general correlations, the seven two-dimensional metric kernels, the
non-saturated multi-trait structures with a relationship matrix, the
separable product $I \otimes \mathrm{AR1} \otimes \mathrm{AR1}$ and the
multi-trait spatial residual (111 checks). Twenty-three of its 26 models
match \pkg{asreml}'s log-likelihood to nine decimals, and the reduced-rank
model to $4 \times 10^{-5}$ (\pkg{remlax} the higher). The three that do not are worth naming, because the diagnosis is the
useful part. On the spherical and circular kernels \pkg{asreml} does not
converge: it oscillates between two iterates on the first and stops in the
first of three hills on the second (local maxima at ranges $5.0$, $7.4$ and
$10.9$). Held at \pkg{remlax}'s parameters, \pkg{asreml} returns
\pkg{remlax}'s log-likelihood to the last digit ($3.823231$ and
$13.875897$), so the two programs compute the same function and only the
stopping point differs. On MA(2) \pkg{asreml} declares convergence with its
parameters still moving by 3.5 percent per iteration and stops $0.033$ below
\pkg{remlax}; asked to hold \pkg{remlax}'s coefficients it re-expresses
them on another scale, so its printed parameters are not the moving-average
coefficients of the standard form and the two optima cannot be compared
point-wise. A REML written out in dense algebra reproduces \pkg{remlax}'s
value at \pkg{remlax}'s parameters in all three cases. The circular kernel
also exposed two defects in \pkg{remlax} itself, both fixed in the same
working tree: a NaN gradient beyond the range that stopped the optimiser at
its first iteration, and a start that ignored the multimodality of range
kernels, now replaced by a sweep of the distance deciles. The checks are left
failing, since they measure agreement with what \pkg{asreml} reports, and
the diagnostic script is kept beside them.

\subsection{What was not measured}

The CPU/GPU parity sweep over 34 serialised designs, which had passed on
2026-09-01 with a relative gap of at most $6.4 \times 10^{-15}$, was not
replayed on 2026-09-28 because no GPU was free; the arithmetic it checks has
not changed, but that is a statement about the code, not a measurement. The
structures with no counterpart in any of the four programs, the free
four-parameter Mat\'ern, user-written kernels and separable products of more
than three factors, rest on closed-form profiles and on the dense reference.

\subsection{A conditioning limit, made deliberate}

The mixed model equations \eqref{eq:mme} contain $R^{-1}$, so the sparse
engine degrades as the residual variance approaches zero. Rather than hope an
optimiser visits that region, the check imposes $\theta$ and reports the
degradation, which is monotone: at $\sigma_e^2 = 3.4 \times 10^{-4}$ the two
engines agree to $3.8 \times 10^{-11}$, and at $3.8 \times 10^{-11}$ they
agree only to $1.7 \times 10^{-4}$. This is structural to the formulation,
and it is why \pkg{asreml} parameterises the ratio of variances instead.
